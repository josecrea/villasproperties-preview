04 · Questions

What buyers ask
around here.

01
Can you really negotiate the price when buying in Arona?

In Arona the average asking price is 4.038 €/m², and the notarised price is 3.138 €/m²: a 22,3 % gap. That's not an automatic discount —the notarised figure mixes coast and inland, and a specific property can sit above or below it— but it does prove that the listed price in this zone isn't the closing price.

02
Which zone of Arona fits my budget?

Within Arona there's a 2,49x difference between Playa de las Américas (6.085 €/m²) and Valle San Lorenzo (2.441 €/m²). With the same money you buy almost double the floor area by moving to a different neighbourhood within the same municipality.

03
Does Arona sell a lot of property?

The Land Registry (Catastro) records 3.441 home sales a year in Arona. That matters more than it seems: where there are many transactions there are many comparables, the price is more reliable, and reselling later is easier.

04
What costs does the buyer pay on top of the price?

On top of the price: transfer tax (ITP) for a resale property —or IGIC and stamp duty for a new build—, the notary, the Land Registry, the gestor (administrative agent) and, if there's a mortgage, the valuation. It's worth working this out with real figures before signing a deposit contract, because it's the item that derails deals at the last minute.

05
Can I buy in Arona if I'm not a resident of Spain?

Yes, there's no restriction on buying. What you do need is an NIE number, a Spanish account for the payments, and to show the source of funds to the notary. If you also need financing, a non-resident starts from a larger deposit than a resident, so it's worth discussing this with the bank before making an offer, not after.

06
How long does a purchase take from offer to signing?

Without financing: a few weeks. With a mortgage, a realistic timeframe is rarely under 45 days from the deposit contract: below that, the risk isn't the bank's, it's losing the deposit. That timeframe is negotiated in the deposit contract, and it's the point where most deals fall through.

07
What should you check before signing the deposit contract?

An up-to-date Land Registry extract (ownership and charges), that the registered description matches reality and the Cadastre, that there's no unregistered building work, the land's planning status, the community fees and the state of the homeowners' association, and who's up to date on the local property tax (IBI). Finding a problem before signing is a negotiation; finding it afterwards is a lawsuit.
