Buying a home
in Tenerife.

10 questions, with real numbers and no promises we couldn't keep.

FAQ

10 Q&A

1. What does buying cost on top of the price?
Transfer tax (ITP) in the Canary Islands: 6.5% on resale homes (on the higher of the price or the «valor de referencia»). On top of that: notary €600–1,200, land registry €400–800, a gestoría €300–600, and an independent lawyer (±1% + IGIC). Budget ±8% overall. Example: €395,000 → ±€31,900. New-build: 7% IGIC + AJD instead of ITP.
2. Do I need an NIE?
Yes, before signing. It's requested at the Spanish consulate in your home country or at the Policía Nacional in Tenerife; with a power of attorney, your lawyer can handle it for you.
3. Do I need to open a Spanish bank account?
Not compulsory: the deed records the means of payment and a transfer from abroad is valid. It's still convenient for direct-debiting IBI (council tax), community fees and utilities.
4. Do I need to travel to sign?
No. A notarised power of attorney in your home country plus an apostille lets your lawyer sign on your behalf. Most buyers still make one trip to view the property.
5. Can I get a mortgage?
Spanish banks typically lend non-residents 60–70% of the lower of the price or the valuation. Plan for 30–40% of your own funds plus the ±8% in costs.
6. What do I pay every year?
IBI (council tax), community fees, utilities, and Spanish non-resident income tax (IRNR, form 210): 19% on 1.1% (or 2%) of the cadastral value. Example: a €120,000 cadastral value → ±€251/year.
7. What about tax back home?
Most buyers still need to declare the purchase (and, later, any rental income or capital gain) to their home tax authority. Spain has a double-taxation treaty with the UK, Romania, Ukraine, Hungary and Poland, among others, so you won't be taxed twice — but check the specific declaration and deadline that applies where you live, since it varies by country.
8. Can I rent it out to tourists (Airbnb)?
Generally not straight away. Canary Islands Law 6/2025: tourist letting is only allowed where the local municipal plan expressly permits it (max. 10% of homes), the property is at least 10 years old, and the licence runs for 5 years; an existing licence is extinguished when the property is sold (transitional provision 1.11). Since 3 April 2025, prior approval by 3/5 of the owners' community is also required (art. 17.12 LPH). Long-term letting remains possible. We make no promise of tourist-rental income.
9. What if I sell later on?
Spain: 19% on the capital gain (EU residents); the buyer withholds 3% of the price as an advance payment (art. 25.2 TRLIRNR), which is recoverable; plus the municipal «plusvalía». Your home country: usually declared pro rata for that tax year.
10. What does Villa's Properties do?
In-person or video viewing, property documentation (land registry extract, community of owners, IBI, energy performance certificate), coordination with your lawyer and the notary, and support in English. Terms: just ask us.
